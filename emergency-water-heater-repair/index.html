How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life

Why Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair Matters

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, has a reason for it. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, tighten a leaking fitting until it cracks, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into something far worse. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution instead of relying on a sniff test, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. You get that answer before any work begins.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
