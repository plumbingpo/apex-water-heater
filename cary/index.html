How Cary Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Cary

Most water heater problems we see in Cary come back to the water itself. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling you may hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Age matters just as much. Many homes in the area still have the original tank, and once a tank is past the ten-year mark, the anode rod has usually been consumed and rust begins attacking the steel itself. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Code requirements add another layer. Replacing an older heater frequently means updating the installation along the way: a thermal expansion tank if the water system is closed, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Homes on well water have their own issues, including extra sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that occasionally shows up when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early.
